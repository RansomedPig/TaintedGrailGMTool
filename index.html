Game Overview & How to Play

+
System: Story Arc System (updated from Shadows of Esteren)
Setting: The island of Avalon, Year 110 (after the Fall)
Players: 3—6 recommended (including the Leader/Narrator)
Genre: Twilight fantasy inspired by Arthurian myth & Celtic legends
Dice: One ten-sided die (1D10) for all rolls

What Is This Game?

+

Tainted Grail is a collaborative storytelling game. One participant is the Leader (narrator/game master) who describes the world and plays all non-player characters. The other participants are Players who each control one Character — an ordinary person called to become a "Guardian of Avalon."

During sessions, Characters explore the island, interact with NPCs, solve problems, and fight enemies. The Leader describes situations and asks Players to roll dice when outcomes are uncertain.

The game takes place on Avalon — a cursed island shrouded in magical fog (the Wyrdness). Humanity is trying to rebuild after 100 years of isolation. Your Characters are destined to become Guardians — protectors imbued with King Arthur's ancient power.

The Core Mechanic (How Rolls Work)

+

Whenever your Character attempts something with uncertain results, you make a Resolution Roll:

Domain (or Discipline) + Way + 1D10 = Difficulty Threshold

Here's what that means:

  • Domain: Your skill rating in the relevant area (0—5). Example: "Close Combat" for sword fighting, "Stealth" for sneaking.
  • Way: One of your five personality traits (1—5) that the Leader says applies. Each Domain has a default Way.
  • 1D10: Roll one ten-sided die and add the result.
  • Difficulty Threshold: The number you must reach or exceed to succeed (set by the Leader).
Example: Maeve wants to climb a wall. The Leader says it's a Feats roll (Standard difficulty = 11). Maeve has Feats 2 and Combativeness 4. She rolls 1D10 and gets a 6. Total: 2 + 4 + 6 = 12. That beats 11, so she succeeds!

If your Domain is 0, you can still try — just use Way + 1D10.

World & Lore Terms

+
The Wyrdness
The primal, untamable power that suffuses Avalon and governs its very existence. Seen mostly as a conscious, shape-shifting fog, it creates, mutates, and destroys without end. See the Wyrdness (Lore) chapter.
Fore-dwellers
The island's native inhabitants and humanity's oldest foe: four-armed, triple-eyed armored titans who wield the Wyrdness like a weapon and appear immune to it. They rule from Tuathan.
Fomorians
An ancient race of giant sea-people who arrived on flagless ships and massacred the coastal villages.
Menhir
Ancient standing stones that Merlin modified to repel the Wyrdness (and weaken the Fore-dwellers). Their gradual failure caused the Fall of Avalon; most now stand depleted.
Guardians of Avalon
Reincarnating essences of raw Wyrd energy seeded by Arthur's sacrifice, which bind to ordinary people when humanity is threatened. The player characters are the new Guardians.
Metamorphosis
The process by which a Guardian grows beyond the human condition, unlocking ever-greater power along their Narrative Arc.
The Pale Lady
Also the Lady of the Lake: an exiled Fore-dweller who gave Arthur his guiding visions and the power to win his war. She dwells beneath Mirror Lake; her purposes are her own.
The Grail
A mythical cup said to grant eternal life, reputedly stolen from the Fore-dwellers by Arthur in the Age of Legends. Now lost, but endlessly sought.
Excalibur
Arthur's enchanted blade, driven into the heart of Tuathan to corrupt the Fore-dwellers' power — the act that seeded the first Guardians.
Order of the Round Table
Arthur's order of knights, based at Kamelot. Corrupted by the Fall, it survives in scattered, diminished form; its fate differs by Vision.
The Red Death
The devastating plague that drove humanity from the Continent and, a year after the Fall, consumed the old capital of Camlann.

The Homelands, Red Death & Exodus

+

For more than a thousand years humanity flourished on the Continent — "the Homelands" — a bastion of trade, art, and arcane learning centered on the great city of Camlann, ruled by King Uther Pendragon and then his son Arthur. That golden age ended when the Red Death arose: a merciless plague of profuse bleeding, purulent cankers, bloody coughing, and rapid organ failure that spread faster than any cure. Whole cities emptied, and centuries of accumulated knowledge — including the fabled library of Il-Beddi — burned or rotted away with the people who kept it.

  • Arthur was visited in a dream by the Pale Lady, who directed him to a new land across the sea, unspoiled by the plague: Avalon
  • He gathered 5,000+ men-at-arms, settlers, and desperate exiles and set sail westward
  • The crossing was brutal — roughly a third of his forces were lost to storms, the Red Death carried aboard, and mutinies born of the strange magical fog that met them near the island
Expanded history: The plague did not stay buried on the Continent. A year after the Fall, it takes the old capital of Camlann and turns it into a dead city rising from the mist. When the Red Death reaches the capital, its people flee on anything that will float, and the great Old Port — whose berths once held ships from every known land — falls silent and empty, its piers half-collapsed. Red banners hang limp over streets sealed off by stockades of sharpened stakes. Through the ruins drifts the Danse Macabre, a horror that sways to unseen pipes and drums, while Plaguebeasts and Fomorian Sentinels prowl the rubble and a thickening Miasma chokes any who delve too deep. Only a handful of refuges — the Votive Temple, the Ramshackle Wharf — still hold out against the death.

The Age of Legends (Conquest)

+
  • Immediate conflict with Fore-dwellers — four-armed armored titans, the island's native inhabitants
  • Merlin modified standing stones (menhirs) to repel Wyrdness and weaken Fore-dwellers
  • Merlin was killed by Fore-dwellers during the war
  • The Pale Lady (an exiled Fore-dweller) aided Arthur and guided humanity's war
  • Arthur learned Wyrdness powers — became mighty but was physically corrupted
  • Arthur drove Excalibur into the heart of Tuathan, weakening the Fore-dwellers
  • Created Guardian essences — beings of raw energy that reincarnate to defend humanity
  • The Grail: A mythical cup said to grant eternal life, stolen from the Fore-dwellers
Expanded history: Humanity comes to Avalon as refugees, not conquerors, and the land is no empty prize — it belongs to the Fore-dwellers, four-armed, triple-eyed titans who wield the Wyrdness like a weapon and shatter the first landings in under a week. The survivors claw out a foothold at the southern tip and press inland through choke-points like the neck of Riverfall, a narrow strip between the sea and the enemy's walls. With the only road running west, Merlin and Galahad lead some five thousand souls on an expedition to map a southern passage around Tuathan — Merlin's spells tearing safe corridors through the fog, while formations like the Red Shields (many already plague-touched) are spent as the tip of the spear. The bloodshed at the Twin Sisters and the Great Dolmen is still sung of. Arthur seizes the Fore-dweller garrison of Wyrdfort and makes it his citadel, and forges his fateful alliance with the exiled Pale Lady.

The Fall of Avalon & Century of Mists

+
  • Menhirs exhausted their power one by one over centuries
  • Civil war erupted; communities raided each other
  • Fore-dwellers returned from Tuathan in force
  • Tuathan unleashed primordial Wyrdness — over half the island consumed in 5 months
  • Orrin (Merlin's last adept) erected the Crude Menhir, saving one stronghold
  • The Century of Mists began — 100+ years of isolation and bare survival
  • Now (Year 110), the mists are receding and humanity is cautiously re-emerging
Expanded history: The collapse begins from within. For six centuries the menhirs hold the fog at bay; then, without warning, the stones start to go dark. The blight begins in the south — around farmholds like Cuanacht, whose failing menhir sends its champions on a doomed expedition into Tuathan — and creeps inward until the whole island lies exposed. Loyalties die with the stones: cities that had answered to Kamelot break away, warlords like Mordred of Crow's Nest carve out fiefs, and holds such as Timberwall and Farshire bleed one another in siege after siege while the Round Table's envoys fail to keep the peace. Then Tuathan disgorges the Fore-dwellers anew and unleashes the primordial Wyrdness held in its bowels, and the eastern territories nearest the enemy capital are swept away in hours — a dying island of warring holds, haunted ruins, and a Lady in the lake who keeps sending heroes to their deaths in Tuathan.

Political Structure & Key Figures

+

After the Century of Mists, Avalon has no single ruler — only a patchwork of communities emerging from isolation. The village remains the basic unit: small, self-sufficient, and population-controlled, governed in many forms from councils of elders to petty tyrants. Above them, a handful of would-be powers vie to fill the vacuum left by Arthur's vanished crown, each claiming a slice of the recolonized island.

The great question of the age is reunification: Kamelot pushes to gather the villages back under one banner, while independent lords and warlords resist — some out of hard-won freedom, others out of naked ambition. The Guardians (the players) walk into the middle of this cold war.

Avalon's Protectors

+

Orders of Chivalry

The chivalric ideal outlived the Fall. Roughly a dozen minor orders rose in the early days of the reconquest to serve as humanity's first line of defense — building and guarding the routes that link the communities, and standing against the horrors of the fog. Shaped by the legacy of the Round Table and the fading courtly customs of the Continent, their knights swear vows of poverty and moral excellence, and the orders are open to all genders. Each holds self-sufficient strongholds (commanderies) and sends representatives to an annual council at the Castle of the Sore Watch, where leadership rotates through the "Perilous Seat" — currently held by Lamorak of the Order of Camlann. The oldest, the Order of Camlann, dates to the Age of Legends and keeps faith with Arthur's teachings; newer ones like the Order of the Bronze Cross won renown just five years ago defending against a Fore-dweller host.

The Wyrdhunters (fewer than 50)

A small, mystical, and selfless congregation devoted to understanding and destroying the aberrations the Wyrdness spawns — a charge they have carried since the Fall, in keeping with Arthur's old goal of ridding the island of monsters. They answer to no lord and pointedly stay out of Avalon's political feuds. Unusually, they recruit for aptitude and affinity rather than martial prowess, welcoming even those the Wyrdness has already touched. Initiates who survive a harrowing ordeal earn the title "Wyrdtracker" and choose a life of wandering and destitution. A village in need summons them by writing its name on a distinctive stela topped with a deer skull; the Hunters then gather and appoint one of their own to answer the call.

The Mistfarers

A corps of explorers who first braved the fog about eight years ago, the Mistfarers are the reason the island is knitting itself back together. Guiding convoys of settlers and traders with Wyrdcandles and rune-carved wagons, they map Avalon, chart safe passages, and reconnect villages that spent a century in isolation. It is dangerous, thankless work — convoys vanish into the mists with grim regularity — but without them there could be no reunification and no reconquest.

Magic Users

  • Merlin's Line (Wizards): Heirs to the protective arcane arts Merlin brought from the Continent — the very magic that once modified the menhirs. Hunted and all but exterminated, perhaps only a couple still live.
  • Druids of Avalon: Keepers of natural balance with a peculiar, intimate bond with death. Their Archdruids and their Cenacle gather at Moonring, and figures like the Archdruid Amergin still guard the old rites.
  • Disciples of Low Magic: Practitioners who commune with animals and emulate the traits of Avalon's fauna, drawing power from the wild rather than the arcane.

Guardians of Avalon

Arthur's final gift. When he drove Excalibur into the heart of Tuathan roughly six centuries ago, his sacrifice seeded beings of raw Wyrd energy — essences that reincarnate down the generations and bind themselves to ordinary people whenever humanity stands on the brink. A Guardian is an unremarkable soul suddenly charged with ancient power, destined to grow beyond the human condition through Metamorphosis. This is what the player characters are: the reborn protectors of a dying world.

Humanity's Enemies

+

The Fore-dwellers

The island's true natives and humanity's oldest foe: four-armed, triple-eyed titans encased in heavy armor, whose third eye lets them see through the mists. Gifted with monstrous strength and a command of Wyrd-magic that even Merlin could not match, they routed Arthur's landing in less than a week and nearly ended the exodus before it began. They appear immune to the Wyrdness — even strengthened by it — and rule from the shifting capital of Tuathan. Driven back and thought imprisoned after the Age of Legends, they returned in force during the Fall and now roam a broken island once more.

The Fomorians

An ancient race of giant sea-people who arrived on flagless ships out of the deep and put the coastal villages to the sword. Less understood than the Fore-dwellers, they are a horror from the water's edge — a reminder that not every threat to Avalon comes from the fog inland.

The Corrupted Wild

The Wyrdness reshapes living things, and Avalon's fauna has become far deadlier than it once was: boars the size of wagons, wolves that hunt in daylight, and worse. Beyond mutated beasts lurk true monsters — wyrms, giants, wailing banshees, and the blood-hungry Dearg Due — and it is these Wyrdness-spawned aberrations that the Wyrdhunters exist to track and destroy.

The Pale Lady (an uncertain power)

Not quite enemy, not quite ally. An exiled Fore-dweller once held captive at the island's heart, the Pale Lady sent Arthur the dream-visions that drew him to Avalon and lent him the power that won his war — at the cost of his humanity. She dwells now beneath Mirror Lake, and her purposes are her own; those who seek her aid should remember she has spent centuries sending champions to their deaths in Tuathan.

The Wyrdness (Lore)

+
The Wyrdness is a primal, untamable power that has suffused Avalon since time immemorial — the island's very existence is bound to it. What people see as the mists is only a fraction of its true nature: a force of ceaseless metamorphosis that shapes, perverts, and obliterates the world without end. Some call it the beginning and end of all things.

The Nature of the Wyrdness

+

The sovereign fog. To most people the Wyrdness is the thick, moving fog that has smothered Avalon for a century — a misty expanse stretching over dozens of miles, carried on the winds and seemingly possessed of a consciousness of its own. It reacts to its surroundings, and witnesses swear they have seen it come alive: near the ground, wispy tentacles reach out to grasp victims and drag them into the haze, while vaporous appendages sink into the earth. It flees the coasts and throws itself inland, its favored ground, so its density is never constant — a traveler may feel only thin threads brushing their cheek one moment and be swallowed by a thick mantle the next.

A thousand faces. The fog is not its only shape. Morning dew is said to carry its power and water every plant on the island. In the cold north, sudden blizzards petrify trees and split stone before vanishing. Inland, iridescent crystals bloom at the edges of villages casting distorted reflections, silver stalactites hang in the foliage and whisper a strange melody in the wind, and marsh lilies breathe out suffocating vapors as caravans pass. No two places suffer the same phenomena, and the Wyrdness has defied every attempt to catalog it.

Never truly defeated. Repellents and the ancient menhirs can disperse it for a time, but it always reforms, welling up as if from an infinite reservoir to reclaim every space it touches. No sane settler travels without a Wyrdcandle or talisman — even to somewhere thought safe.

Effects on the World

+
  • On living things: physical mutation, mental alteration, and the erasure or rewriting of memory. A touch can reshape a creature entirely; the worst afflicted become the Wyrd-claimed.
  • On the land: trees grow upside down, stones float, time itself runs strangely; crystals, stalactites, and petrified groves mark where it has settled.
  • On people: humans are far more sensitive to it than beasts, while the Fore-dwellers appear immune — even strengthened — by its presence.
  • Density: thickest inland and around Tuathan; thinner near the coasts, which it avoids.
A blessing and a curse. The Wyrdness is not purely destructive. Passing through it can bring ruin — decay that rots supplies and turns metal to clay, ambushes by corrupted creatures, a violent "rewrite" of a person's very nature — but it can also grant epiphanies, heightened senses, unnatural bounty, or whispered truths worth hard-won experience. It gives and takes without reason.

Humanity Against the Wyrdness (Defenses)

+
  • Menhirs — Merlin's modified standing stones, humanity's great shield; most are now depleted, and their failure is what doomed Avalon
  • Wyrdcandles and rune-carved wagons for travel through the fog
  • Druidic rituals, Oghamic Marks, and protective talismans
  • The Circle of Protection spell (Sorcery)
  • Repellents that lower the local Wyrdness level around a party

Campaign Creation (Part 2)

+

How It Works

+

At the start of a campaign, players collectively vote on the state of Avalon using 8 Obol tokens each and 3 dice (values 1, 2, 3). 8 questions about the world are posed, and players allocate tokens to influence outcomes. The results determine which "Vision" of Avalon shapes the campaign.

Each Vision provides Bequests (character creation bonuses) and Repercussions (world changes).

The Three Visions

+

During the Creation of Avalon, the group's votes retell how the Guardians of old acted during the Fall — and that retelling settles which Vision of Avalon the campaign inherits. Each Vision reshapes the present-day island: which cities survived, who holds power, and how the world greets the new Guardians. It also grants every character a set of Bequests (creation bonuses) reflecting that history. The three official Visions are quick pre-set outcomes; a group that plays the full Chronicle can land somewhere in between.

VisionThemeOne-line
1: Power to the PeopleFreedom, survivalThe people threw off Kamelot; most communities self-govern
2: The Throne of KamelotOrder, authorityMorgaine united the island under one banner; the old order holds
3: Paths Less TraveledMystery, paradoxThe Guardians walked away; Avalon grew strange and contradictory

Vision 1 — Power to the People ("Freedom will prevail")

What happened: The Guardians of old sought the Lady of the Lake's counsel, overthrew the corrupted Order of the Round Table, and in Tuathan sided with Arthur to keep the status quo and break the cycle of violence. As the Wyrdness storm broke, they returned to Cuanacht, drove off the scavenging lords, and led the villagers to safety.

What it means for the world: Common people rejected their old protectors and seized power. Most communities now self-govern and only a few lords cling to authority. Cuanacht and the Serene Visage survived and thrive as protected Cities; Kamelot limped out of the Mists as a struggling Refuge, its regent hiring thugs and mercenaries. The old War-of-Three-Lords holds (Timberwall, Crow's Nest, Farshire) are ruined but ripe for recolonization.

What it means for the players: The Guardians are folk heroes to the common people — but Kamelot's vassals are hostile, and if Morgaine learns who the characters are she sends a lieutenant to turn their allies against them. Bequests lean toward the common touch and raw survival (bonus Communication with common folk, a choice of Domain boosts, and a generous slug of Experience or extra Survival).

Vision 2 — The Throne of Kamelot ("Order will triumph")

What happened: The Guardians of old helped the throne crush the rebel lords, found the Grail, restored the Round Table, and pushed into Tuathan — where they opposed Arthur and kept Excalibur in its prison, dealing the Fore-dwellers a decisive blow. In the final hours they plundered neighboring provinces to keep Kamelot standing against the fog.

What it means for the world: Temporal power won. Queen Morgaine reinforced her rule and united most of the island under one banner; the New Kamelot and the First Farmhold stand as protected Cities and traditional feudal order endures. But there are scars — Farshire is a plundered graveyard, the Lady of the Lake was so offended she swore never to aid humanity again (Mirror Lake is locked at maximum Wyrdness), and vengeful Fore-dwellers have planted an outpost on the map.

What it means for the players: The Crown and the restored Knights of the Round Table may aid the characters, but the common people are hostile to Guardians. Bequests reflect a knightly, ordered upbringing (Mounted Combat / Inspiration / Healing or Erudition, Magic or Crown-facing skills, a boost to Mental Resistance or Potential, plus the standard Experience).

Vision 3 — Paths Less Traveled ("Humanity will be reborn")

What happened: The Guardians of old broke from the Crown and courted unlikely allies — Mordred of Crow's Nest and the Lady of the Lake. To win Orrin's help and breach Tuathan, they preserved the Round Table as eternal protectors. But the trip through the Wyrd shook them: they emerged to find the island aflame and their allies at each other's throats, and chose to abandon Avalon for more hospitable shores.

What it means for the world: Left to itself through the Century of Mists, Avalon grew into a land of contrasts and paradoxes. Crow's Nest stands empty, reclaimed by nature and stalked by stone golems; Timberwall is an open-air necropolis choked with wyrms where Survival points fail; the First Farmhold is denned by a pair of Scarlet Wyrms. Yet the Round Table kept its honor, and its Tombs (the Dolorous Guard) offer sanctuary and teaching to the worthy.

What it means for the players: The world meets the characters with cold, wary hospitality — mention a "higher purpose" and doors close. Morgaine holds an old grudge but has bigger worries. Bequests favor the mystic and self-reliant (Wyrdness Mysteries or Perception/Monsters, Stealth & Religion or extra Magic, Mental Resistance, plus Experience and a Survival point).

In short: Vision 1 makes you champions of a free people against a bitter Crown; Vision 2 makes you agents of a restored order distrusted by commoners; Vision 3 drops you into a strange, half-abandoned island where almost no one is truly on your side. The vote you cast in Session 0 decides which Avalon you'll fight for.

Resolution System — How It Works

+

Step-by-Step: Making a Roll

+
  1. The Leader decides an action is uncertain enough to require a roll.
  2. The Leader tells you which Domain applies.
  3. If you have a relevant Discipline (specialization rated 6—15), use that instead of the Domain.
  4. Add your Domain/Discipline rating + the associated Way rating + 1D10.
  5. Compare to the Difficulty Threshold. Equal or higher = success!
Domain (or Discipline) + Way + 1D10 = Difficulty Threshold
Example: Maeve wants to climb a wall. Leader says "Feats roll, Standard difficulty (11)." Maeve has Feats 2, Combativeness (associated Way) 4. She rolls 1D10 and gets 6. Total: 2 + 4 + 6 = 12. Beats 11 ? success!
Example (Domain 0): If Maeve had Feats 0, she'd just roll Combativeness 4 + 1D10. Still possible but harder!

Critical Success & Critical Failure

+

Critical Success

Roll a natural 10 ? roll again ? if that's ALSO a 10 ? Critical Success! The die counts as 15 for that roll.

Critical Failure

Roll a natural 1 ? roll again ? if that's ALSO a 1 ? Critical Failure! Results in a 5-point penalty to the roll OR a dramatic negative effect.

Teamwork, Contested Actions & Automatic Success

+

Teamwork (outside combat only)

One Character makes the roll. Each helper sacrifices their action and adds an extra D10 to the roll. The roller keeps the BEST D10 result.

Contested Actions

Both sides roll normally (Domain + Way + 1D10). Highest total wins. Tie = draw.

Automatic Success

If Domain + Way = Difficulty Threshold, no roll needed — you automatically succeed.

Automatic Failure

If even a Critical Success (die = 15) can't reach the threshold, or a required Discipline is missing, the action automatically fails.

How to Attack & Calculate Damage

+
Attack Roll: Combativeness + Combat Domain + Stance Modifier + 1D10

If Attack Roll > opponent's Defense ? HIT!

Damage: Attack Roll + Weapon Damage - Opponent's Defense - Opponent's Protection

Each damage point fills one box on the target's Health track.

Parry, Lead & Other Actions

+

Parry (Active Defense)

Must be in Standard or Defensive stance. Costs your action. Roll: Combativeness + Close Combat + Stance Mod + 1D10 vs. attacker's Attack roll. If you win ? no damage.

Lead (Leadership in Combat)

Costs your action. Roll Leadership (Standard 11). Allies within 30 ft get a bonus for 2 Rounds: result 11—13 = +1, 14—16 = +2, 17—20 = +3, 21+ = +4.

Inspire (Rally Fleeing Allies)

Roll Inspiration (Standard 11). Allies within 60 ft get MR bonus: 11—13 = +1, 14—16 = +2, 17—20 = +3, 21+ = +5.

How to Cast a Spell (Step by Step)

+
  1. Choose a spell you know and check its MP cost and casting time.
  2. Spend the MP cost. If you don't have enough, you can't cast it.

  4. Roll: Discipline + Associated Way + 1D10 = Modified Difficulty.
